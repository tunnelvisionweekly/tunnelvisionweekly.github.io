Interview of Jordan, GM Candidate

Published 3/24/26 | Tunnel Vision Weekly #16

By I

Jordan, a candidate for the Union's next GM, and posterer of the JEC–DCC tunnels recently, agreed to an interview with us. Read what he's got to say below!

What are the core values you're going to be focused on as GM?

There's three core values that I intend on focusing on as GM. First of all, transparency. Students deserve to know what their government is actually doing and why. It's why I grew @rpistugov to 4 million views and shifted our focus to make 'brainrot content.' I wanted Student Government to be incredibly visible in the eyes of the students, and I hated the fact that our current marketing felt like advertising. Second of all is pride. I want students to be proud of this school, not in just what we are currently, but what we can be. RPI has 200 years of innovation behind it, an alum is about to command humanity's return to the Moon, we're responsible for so much technological innovation across America. We can have so many cool traditions, a strong culture of arts & music, more love for our community. We should take pride in that vision.

You've utilized Instagram Reels and philanthropic actions during this campaign. What was your philosophy behind this? Have you seen any advantages from this approach?

The philosophy is pretty simple: meet students where they are. Most students aren't reading lengthy campaign platforms, or looking at every single poster. They're on their phones scrolling between classes. If I can't make my message work in that format, then I'm not communicating well enough.

The reels also serve a bigger point as a proof of concept for my intended goals. I'm running on a platform that includes building a student-led 'Rensselaer Media Coalition' to tell RPI's story to the world. The idea is to get students to create reels for channels like @rpiofficial and @rpialumni, while building up their own club social media presence. If I can't demonstrate that I know how to create content that people actually engage with, why would anyone trust me to build that out?

As for the philanthropic side (like through the tunnel art, or through the WD-40 chair stunt), I'm doing it because I have the firm belief that if there's something I want at RPI, I should go out and make it happen. I'm trying to set an example that the administration's failure to fix the DCC chairs, or destruction of the tunnel as a living art project can't and won't stop RPI students. We're more resilient than that, and by running for a high elected position while taking these risks (which may upset admin), I think I'm setting up a good example for the rest of the student body (also my chair squeaked during an exam once and everybody was staring at me).

What's one thing you'd want to change or improve at this school, and what would your first steps be?

The disconnect between students and their own school. Right now most students experience RPI as a place you suffer through to get a degree and then leave. It feels like a transaction, not a core identity. I want students to feel like they're part of something they're genuinely proud of, and I want students to brag to their friends that they go to RPI.

First steps: within the fall semester, I'd hold the first semesterly Town Hall. Students, Senate, and administration in the McNeil room, with a 'State of the Union' address followed up by an open-mic format with an audience questioning. Administrators and Senate would be held accountable, and take feedback directly from students. And I'd begin recruiting student creators from a variety of clubs (many of which have expressed interest to collaborate w/ StuGov already), to launch the Rensselaer Media Coalition, so we start telling our own story instead of waiting for an administration PR office that hasn't gotten the job done.

How would you communicate back to students what's actually happening in meetings with the administration?

I'd like to leverage the GM's power to email students more—I feel like it was severely underutilized for a majority of the semester and only really brought up in random scenarios like during the constitutional amendment. By leveraging things like the email, and also continuing social media engagement, I think it's possible to get a lot of people in the loop about what administration currently is doing. Furthermore, my plan to establish semesterly town halls heavily helps with this, where administrators not only are asked about what they're currently doing, but also can answer questions from students to clarify policy issues and campus-wide changes.

How do you make sure you're representing the views of your peers and not just your own opinions?

I think that having good intentions alone isn't really ever enough for this. You can say you'd represent the views of students, but in reality, everyone has biases, even in Student Government. Our perspectives are different than those of someone in a fraternity or someone in athletics. We have to build better systems for direct outreach, and be proactive. Dormstorming was exactly this: we went directly into freshman dorms and asked students what they cared about instead of assuming we knew. The Town Halls I'm proposing are the same idea scaled up, a visible open-invite format. Through a highly publicized event, we can get more feedback and perspective than ever before by simply putting ourselves out there, and ingraining ourselves in Union culture. I also think the @rpistugov Instagram proved that when you take yourselves less seriously, students care more.

Anything else you want to say to the people of the underground?

The GM sets a focus and direction of the Senate, and meets with administrators, but ultimately it comes down to the committee chairs and senators directly to make our vision come true. That's where all of you come in. Within the first week, there are plenty of positions open for committee chair roles, where, if I'm elected, you can be a direct member of my cabinet.

I won't be looking for experience, or prior knowledge, but rather passion and willingness to learn. I began my journey in Student Government literally a month into my freshman year, with absolutely no idea what I was doing. But I cared enough, and a little more than a year later, I have the privilege to be running for this role. So please, apply to committee chair roles, and get involved. I'm running a campaign that will need a variety of passionate individuals who genuinely care about this school, and want it to be better.
