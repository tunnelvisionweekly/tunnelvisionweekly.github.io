What Happened to the Old Building by the Garage?

Published 2/2/26 | Tunnel Vision Weekly #14

By I

At what point does a building deserve to be acknowledged? This is the question I found myself asking after the building that once stood on 95 College Avenue was torn down over break. It was the building by the RPI parking garage, all ornate and old-looking. Turns out it was very close to being the oldest building on campus! I would not be surprised if you haven't seen it though, it was a pretty small building. Not to mention the fact that RPI has no mention of this building anywhere on its websites or campus maps.

This building, according to the Times Union, stood for around 111 years, standing on RPI campus for at least 50–70. Built initially for a businessman's estate in the late 1800s/early 1900s by architect Alexander Jackson Davis, it was relocated off of the site of where Prospect Park is now sometime during the park's creation.

Around November 2025, an orange plastic construction fence was placed around the structure, as it had been condemned due to structural failures. They were pretty visible, a side wall was pretty heavily bowing out. Then, over winter break, it was completely torn down, leaving only dead grass in its wake. What's unfortunate is preservationists in Troy had been trying to save the building for some time now, with RPI doing nothing to fix up the building. By the time it was condemned, it was reported that it would cost over $1,000,000 to fix back up.

On a post on the r/RPI subreddit, people shared their dismay of the demolition, one saying they were "heartbroken," with others also voicing their unhappiness over this change. Some speculated what happened to it, with a leading theory being the RPI nuclear engineers had a bit of an oopsie. Another theory is that someone ate it. Who knows for sure? It happened over break, after all.

The demolition of the small gatehouse on 95 College Avenue was done without much mention of its impending removal, the final nail in the coffin for a building that sat unacknowledged for decades. It's a landmark that probably thousands of students have seen on their way to classes or cars, and yet RPI hasn't made any mention of its removal. At least an email could have been nice.
