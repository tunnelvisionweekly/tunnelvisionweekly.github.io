The Inaugural Touch Grass Music Festival

Published 4/21/26 | Tunnel Vision Weekly #18

By I

Did you go to the RPI Touch Grass Music Festival on April 11th? If you didn't, you missed out! Don't worry though, we here at Tunnel Vision don't want you to feel left out, so here's a little recap!

It all started in the wee hours of the morning for the staff. Turns out they were there from around 5 or 6 AM all the way to 3 AM the next day for setup and takedown. The main events started at 3, though. And the music went from ~4:30 to ~11. I for one wasn't expecting to stay longer than a few hours, but I ended up being there from 3 all the way to the end of the last set!

There was lots to do at this festival, besides the obvious pastime of touching grass. If you weren't there for music, there were 2 spots for chalk drawings and a table covered in paper for people to draw on with crayons, tons of crafts vendors, swag as well of course, and plenty of other games and food you could choose from! Some of the promotional material said there were over 40 booths, and I'll believe it! Some of my favorites were the DDR stand, Ben and Jerry's unexpected return via ice cream cart, the WRPI stand with lots of free CDs, and the various food vendors (though some prices were a bit steep, looking at you $10 hot dog). Other fun things to do included carnival games with free prizes, Habitat For Humanity's nail hammering setup, RPI Game Dev Club's custom arcade cabinet, and a whole lot of crafts you could buy from other fellow students!

Music-wise, it was cool to see the crowd ebb and flow as the day turned into night. At the start, there was actually nobody dancing. The pit in front of the stage was completely empty, save for a few people. Most were sitting down, ahem, touching grass. As time went on, however, the sitting crowd thinned out and the pit crowd grew. It reached a crescendo during Pseudosky's set (my personal favorite!), and after that the crowd shrank to perhaps half of what it was by the time Mall Goth, the last band, played. One of my favorite crowd things, besides the numerous mosh pits was a game of Simon Says during Pseudosky's set. Haven't seen that one before!

I personally feel like RPI needs a lot more of these kinds of events. I haven't seen such a congregation of students since that one guy ate an entire rotisserie chicken in front of Sage last school year, and it's a great feeling to be around such a horde of familiar people. You never know when you'll bump into someone you know!

There was so much going on at this festival it was hard to squeeze it all into one page. If you have any photos of the event you'd like to share, send them to us and we may feature it in a future edition or our website!
