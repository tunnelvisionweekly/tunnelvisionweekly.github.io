    There's no medicine to save you from geology!

    Published 10/6/25 | Geologic Fever | Tunnel Vision Weekly #9

    By S. S. Illite

    Little things count just as much as the massive. Geology is a crazy but awfully dynamic argument for size not mattering nearly as much.

    Geologically speaking, we are brand new. Babies. Not in a demeaning manner, but in relating to the sheer magnitude of the geologic timescale, the National Park Service put it simply:

    "This analogy highlights the relative length of each geologic time period. Spread your arms wide. With the span of your arms representing all geologic time, look at one hand; your fingertips represent the formation of Earth and the beginning of geologic time."

    It is practically unfathomable to perceive something so comparably miniscule. We are maybe a fleck of skin, if not a proton of an atom of a cell. We rely so heavily on our own perceptions to move through life, yet how do we move through life trying to perceive the practically imperceivable?

    We study. We conduct experiments, we take notes, we teach, we observe, and we speak out for what's important. Geology is a vital science, and it being such an underrepresented science can be detrimental to the efforts of our scientists. Vitality commands all efforts, no matter how big or small; when vitality is questioned, efforts tend to cease.

    Representation is important, and that's how we share knowledge. And that's the goal of this column! To teach and develop skills with the community is a privilege.
