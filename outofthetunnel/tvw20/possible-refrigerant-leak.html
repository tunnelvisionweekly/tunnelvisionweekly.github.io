Possible Refrigerant Leak Evacuates JEC and DCC

Published 10/04/26 | Out of the Tunnel | Tunnel Vision Weekly #20

By I

Something unusual happened on September 28th this year. I'm not sure if this is the best section to write this in, but it is technically out of the tunnel. Anyway, I personally was heading to the JEC around 4 PM on that day, and when I got to the JEC, I heard an alarm blaring in the loading dock and some people, including PubSafe and what looked like some building managers, were talking. I saw some people waiting outside, and relatively quickly some fire trucks (including a hazmat truck?), an ambulance, and the fire marshal arrived on scene.

Initially, nobody knew what was going on, and there was only a handful of people outside. People were just standing outside in the drizzling rain or inside the JEC wind tunnel. After a few minutes, it appeared that the situation was concluded and it was probably just a drill, but that was far from the truth.

A minute or two after the first fire trucks were told they could leave, another fire truck arrived. This time, it was just them, but with this it was clear something more was happening. The first responders had talks and walked in and out of the JEC for some time, before all of a sudden, the DCC of all places, sounded its fire alarm. It sounded like an old school bell, and it was persistent. The fire department had to try multiple times to shut it off, eventually getting it turned off on something like the 4th or 5th try. It was around this time that the JEC was fully evacuated, and many more people were now outside. There was a bustle and general feeling of confusion in the air. Like the Titanic, there was also a band playing. For a few more minutes firefighters went in and out of the DCC and JEC, and we were all still in the dark on what was going on.

Finally, the RPI alert was issued around 5 PM, letting everyone know that there was a potential refrigerant leak in the buildings and they had to be evacuated. It took at least an hour, closer to 2 if counting the time that the first fire trucks arrived. By the end, the crowd had thinned to almost zero.

It appears that there was no injury or anything during this whole ordeal, thankfully, and those who had classes during this time probably got some extra free time that day. Unfortunately, no more public statements on the ordeal have been released as of 10/01, so we may never know if the potential refrigerant leak was real or a false alarm.
