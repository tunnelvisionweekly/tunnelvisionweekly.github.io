Folsom Fun!

Published 4/9/2026 | Out of the Tunnel | Tunnel Vision Weekly #17

By West Virginia

The Richard Gilman Folsom Library was completed in 1976. Students and professors alike have strong opinions on this eye-catching building. I've never been a fan of brutalism, especially on an academic campus, and I have directed many an insult at Folsom for that exact reason. But, over time, I've found that it's grown on me.

Across its 4 floors it houses over 400,000 physical volumes, over 200,000 electronic books, 66 private study rooms, and an up-to-date archive of thousands of federal documents open to the public. Yet it only has 3 water fountains. Technically the first floor has the café, so you could get water there, but as for water fountains specifically, there are none to be found.

Let's work our way up starting at the basement level, which is technically the first floor. This fountain is in the same location you'll find all of the Folsom fountains. It's to the left of the elevator, just around the corner from the doors. Starting off strong, the basement fountain is mildly cold and has enough pressure to fill most water bottles up, assuming you tilt it heavily.

Jumping up to the third floor you'll find a much colder fountain, with the trade-off being a weaker stream.

Last but not least, the 4th floor water fountain has a stream almost identical to that of its basement level counterpart; however, this particular water fountain has a special hidden feature. Its nozzle is loose, which allows the user to rotate it, so your bottle filling is not obstructed by the basin. I gotta say, it is very fun to fill your water bottle up this way, and it's almost enough to declare this the best fountain in Folsom, but unfortunately the fourth floor always has the warmest water in the whole building. That's why my pick is the third floor. Although it has the weakest stream, its icy temperatures make up for it, and combined with the orange carpets, the third floor experience is truly what makes it the best fountain in Folsom.
