DCC 308, A Case Study

Published 9/23/25 | Out of the Tunnel | Tunnel Vision Weekly #8

By West Virginia

DCC 308. The quintessential RPI lecture hall. Chances are you've had at least one class in this room during your time here. My first ever class at RPI was Intro to Biology in DCC 308. Upon first glance DCC 308 is a regular lecture hall set up with uniform yellow plaster chairs. In reality it's a regular lecture hall with non-uniform yellow plaster chairs!

Back in 1973 when the DCC was first built, 308 had one less row of seats, a bar separating students from the lecture floor, and the lecture floor was about a foot lower. The lecture hall is comprised of three types of yellow plaster seats. Type One features a fully square back and square seat. Type Two features a slightly rounded back, no longer fully square, with a seat that is a slightly more rounded version of the Type One seat. Honorary mention for the only non-yellow seat. A gray colored Type Two seat can be found in the upper rightmost corner of 308, when facing the room from the lecture stand. Type Three features the most triangular back of the three. The type three bottom also features less of a concave shape when compared to Types One and Two.

So what seat should you choose? Right away you should know Type Three chairs are generally thinner than the others and will offer limited back support. That being said, if you like bouncing back and forth in your seat, a Type Three chair might just be exactly what you're looking for. Type one chairs feature a bottom with a comparatively larger surface area if you like a large seat, but the lip of the chair paired with the depth of the seat can result in cut-off blood circulation to the legs when seated for extended periods of time. Type Two are my personal favorite. The perfect depth and lip will leave you feeling comfortably secure in the chair without your legs falling asleep, and the back is sturdy without being too hard. So now armed with this information, I trust you will pick the best seat for you!
