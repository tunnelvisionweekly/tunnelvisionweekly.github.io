boygenius Review

Published 11/17/25 | Subterranean Homesick Blues | Tunnel Vision Weekly #12

By π

Indie supergroup boygenius' self-titled EP feels like huddling in the after-hours glow of a vintage jukebox, three friends bleeding their hearts into a microphone in the corner of a basement club. It balances on the knife's edge of innocence and intensity, like a comforting hug after feeling alone for too long. The trio (Julien Baker, Phoebe Bridgers, and Lucy Dacus) each bring their own scars, weaving them into six songs that sound like late-night confessions. By turns folky, raucous, and quietly epic, the EP could be the soundtrack to your most vulnerable moments.

The name boygenius itself is a coy wink and upside-down joke at all the overzealous "boy geniuses" that we all had the (dis)pleasure of meeting. These three grew up in small towns and quiet churches, raised on heavy silences and hidden truths, and formed a bond on subtle guitar riffs and silly giggles late into the night.

In "Bite the Hand," Lucy Dacus's rich contralto sings against a steady guitar, the notes pulsing like a defiant heartbeat. By the chorus all three voices converge on the refrain "I can't love you how you want me to," turning a quiet confession into a impassioned testimony. What starts as a whisper becomes a collective cathartic scream.

With "Me & My Dog," Phoebe Bridgers takes the mic with her trademark deadpan lyricism. The song sounds sweet and jangly, but the humor is bitter: every line lands like a clever barb at loneliness. Phoebe's soft drawl half-smiles through heartbreak, making solitude sound like the punchline of an inside joke.

"Souvenir" is the song I'm quietly obsessed with. A love confession from someone too insecure and somber to know if they are capable of love, or being loved. It floats somberly along in a cloudy haze, tender and hesitant, like a feeling you wake up from and wish you could fall back into.

In "Stay Down," Julien Baker continues this theme by reflecting on her feelings of isolation. Guitars swirl around her vocals like a cyclone, chords heavy under the weight of her pain. "Push me down into the water… hold me under," she cries, unable to properly cope with the breakup she stays in the murk that the emptiness has left her in.

Then "Salt in the Wound," led by Lucy Dacus, plunges everything right back in. Her guitar work slices through the air and her voice matches the acidity in her lyricism. "They say the Hearts and Minds are on your side…what they don't say is what's on your side." Her callous honesty perfectly harmonizes with the raw instrumentation that swells behind her.

Finally, "Ketchum, ID" closes the EP in a wash of calm. It's a road-trip lullaby by Phoebe: just guitar, harmonies, and a gentle pedal steel. They dream of escaping to snowy hills and quiet cabins—"so far from where we've been," their vocals promise, finally a place to catch your breath. It feels like a calm bus ride home through Massachusetts or a warm hug after crying from homesickness; soft, melancholy, and undeniably sincere.

What makes boygenius' EP so powerful is its vulnerability. Its intimacy is sometimes overwhelming, like every word is written in cursive and smudged with tear stains. The boys' emotional bond is evident, three voices turning loneliness into something shared. It's friendship blazing bright in the dark: an anthem for anyone who's ever whispered their true self into the night.
