Vroom Vroom Review

Published 10/6/25 | Subterranean Homesick Blues | Tunnel Vision Weekly #9

By π

Explosive and unapologetic, Charli XCX's Vroom Vroom, released in 2016, is not just an EP, it's a prophecy. Across its brief 4 song tracklist, Charli tears away from her mainstream (and honestly dull) pop image and launches herself into the bombastic hyperpop soaked sound that she would come to perfect. Produced primarily by SOPHIE, the late great visionary behind some of the most forward-thinking electronic music of the 2010s, Vroom Vroom is abrasive, metallic, and unapologetically synthetic. It represents a shift not only in Charli's career, but for pop music as a whole marking the moment where the underground PC Music aesthetic collided with the mainstream and redeﬁned what "pop music" could sound like.

The opening and title track, "Vroom Vroom," sets the tone immediately: robotic bass lines, distorted percussion, and Charli's deadpan delivery combine into a sound that feels like it's been beamed from a neon-lit, cyberpunk dream. "Let's ride," she commands, turning a car into a metaphor for control, power, and self-possession. The lyrics are simple, even absurd at times, but don't let that fool you. Charli's icy conﬁdence and tongue-in-cheek vocals make it clear she knows exactly what she's doing. It's ironic, it's aggressive, and it's addictive.

"Trophy" continues the adrenaline rush, with glitchy punchy drums and sharp-edged synths that sound like a digital engine revving. SOPHIE's ﬁngerprints are unmistakable here as the track shifts and mutates constantly, blending sweetness and chaos into something that feels mechanical yet danceable. Charli sounds like she's performing inside a titanium spaceship as her chipmunked vocals soar over the anodic snares and prominent vocal samples from Quentin Tarantino's Kill Bill.

"Paradise" offers a rare glimpse of warmth, featuring Hannah Diamond's breathy vocals over a glossy, dreamlike beat that describes love in the digital metascape. It is synthetic, ﬂeeting, and strangely innocent.

The closing track, "Secret (Shh)," wraps things up with whispery vocals and rubbery baselines that feel both playful and sinister. Over synths blaring like perverse sirens, Charli tells us how she is ready to lose control and that she will only tell us how far she will go if we promise to keep her secret. It's seductive and sleek while still complimenting the baleful energy of SOPHIE's production.

What makes Vroom Vroom so compelling is how boldly it rejects convention. It's not radio-friendly nor approachable and it's not meant to be. Instead, it embraces artiﬁciality, noise, and synthetic excess to form a beautiful glitchy cacophony. The reception of this EP upon release was mixed, it received critical praise amongst PC Music fans, but many Angles found themselves unsure of what to make of her new sound. Fast-forward to today and this album has served as a launching point for the explosion of hyperpop music that followed.

In just sixteen minutes, Charli XCX dismantled her own cliche pop star image and rebuilt it from silicon and steel. Vroom Vroom serves as the sound of pop's future arriving early and unapologetically. It remains one of the most important and thrilling releases of her career, a perfect collision of bubblegum and chaos that still sounds ahead of its time.
