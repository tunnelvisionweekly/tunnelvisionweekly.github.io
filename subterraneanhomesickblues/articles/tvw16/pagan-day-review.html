Pagan Day Review

Published 3/24/26 | Subterranean Homesick Blues | Tunnel Vision Weekly #16

By π

Montréal's psych-punk supergroup PyPy's (pronounced π π) debut Pagan Day is like a fever dream laced with battery acid. It is a seven-track odyssexy filled with fuzzy riffs, primal howling, and psychedelic breakdowns that feel like a séances that is escaping through your drivers.

PyPy is comprised of Montréal's most unhinged pedigrees: Roy Vucino (guitar/vocals) of Red Mass and CPC Gangbangs infamy, Annie-Claude Deschênes (vocals) with Philippe Clement (bass) and Simon Besré (drums) from Duchess Says. Four members from four corners of Montréal's underground, converging on a sound that's irrational, transcendent, and—like the constant they're named after—never-ending and impossible to grasp.

The title track launches the record like a black mass at a house show. Vucino's guitar snarls through sheets of distortion while Deschênes's vocals rise like necromantic scripture over Besré's relentless kit work. Its foreboding tone perfectly sets the tone for a record that refuses to travel in a straight line.

"New York" launches forward with a Stranglers-inspired base line and a sense of sleaze that could strip the paint off a bathroom wall. But the real curveball comes with "Daffodils." Here the band slips into a gothy subdued groove. With strong disco-breaks and driving bassline, it slithers and hypnotizes you like digits spiraling out past the decimal with no end in sight.

"Too Much Cocaine" is exactly what it sounds like: a sweaty, teeth-grinding ode to excess. The instruments grinding and lurching against one another in jittery, paranoid lockstep. And then "She's Gone" explodes like a bomb. It's blistering pop that descends into unhinged prog-shred excess, mangling every last drop of juice from a wah pedal until it begs for mercy.

The record closes with the sprawling "Ya Ya Ya / Psychedelic Overlords," a two-headed demon that allows the band to stretch into a full-on lysergic communion. Its riffs circle on and on, while the whole thing ascends into a punk-psych blur. A trance that drones to infinity.

Pagan Day is a simply excellent debut. It is a sweat-soaked, lo-fi invocation that proves the most dangerous sounds are the ones you can't quite compute. My advice is to just round up. You'll never get it exact anyway.
