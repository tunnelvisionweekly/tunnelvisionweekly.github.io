City of Doves Review

Published 2/3/26 | Subterranean Homesick Blues | Tunnel Vision Weekly #14

By π

Sickboyrari's City of Doves unfolds like a fog-drenched dream drifting through empty streets at dawn. Its production is beautiful, bleak, and heavy with unspoken weight. The record is woozy, almost understated sometimes. Atmosphere, leaning into ghostly synths, muted trap percussion, and melodies that ebb in and out rather than being tied down by a structured chorus.

Rari's voice is the anchor and the apparition at once. Slurred, auto-tuned, and emotionally distant, it glides across the production with a sense of detachment with intentional carelessness. His drugged-out delivery makes his perspective feel less like a solid recollection but a dreary hypnogenic vestige of his memories. His delivery never forces attention; it invites the listener inward, into a private world of loss, paranoia, and spiritual unrest.

The album's themes orbit loneliness, addiction, faith, and the hollowness of material success. Designer labels, street mythology, angels, and demons blur together into a single language of excess and regret. Rari catalogs his pain, painting with texture over sputtering high hats each fading memory of his life of excess. There's a quiet desperation beneath the surface, a sense that every track serves more as meditation rather than a proclamation.

Production throughout City of Doves remains restrained and immersive. Beats pulse softly, often dissolving before they fully assert themselves, creating a floating sensation that mirrors dissociation. The music rarely peaks; instead, it drifts, allowing sadness and introspection to stretch out and linger. The album is icy cold, the space between Rari and his production reflects the dissonance between Rari and his environment.

By the end, City of Doves, like a lot of the best hip-hop projects, entrances you into Rari's state of mind. It captures the ache of existing between worlds: fame and anonymity, faith and nihilism, numbness and excess. A pioneer in his sound with a project that serves as a Rosetta Stone for trap music of the last decade.
