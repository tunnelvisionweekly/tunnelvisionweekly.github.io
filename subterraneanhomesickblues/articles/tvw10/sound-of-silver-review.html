    Sound of Silver Review

    Published 10/20/25 | Subterranean Homesick Blues | Tunnel Vision Weekly #10

    By π

    LCD Soundsystem is the musical collective and love child of multi-instrumentalist, singer-songwriter, DJ, and producer James Murphy. On the band's sophomore album Sound of Silver, Murphy, along with his tight-knit group of collaborators, builds something that feels both deeply personal yet seeped in Murphy's own inﬂuences, like a love letter made of all his favorite records. Murphy has never shied from where he draws his inﬂuences from, but his meticulous curation of new wave, funk, punk, and house, or as he calls it, "silver music," gives the album its unmistakable pulse. The whole thing is soaked in groovy baselines, wiry distorted synths, and rhythms that sound equally at home on a hi-ﬁ setup in a Brooklyn loft or in a dingy smoke-ﬁlled club in Cologne.

    There's something almost cruel about how Sound of Silver balances irony and sincerity. It's Murphy at his most self-aware, funny, and devastating. The record feels like the hangover at the end of your 30s, the realization that chasing cool doesn't make you feel alive anymore and groovy baselines and driving drums are all that's left to keep you moving.

    "Get Innocuous!" opens the record like a trance, all shimmering synths and mechanical pulse, slowly building until it devours all of the space around you. With haunting montras it perfectly sets the tone for the record. "Time to Get Away" and "North American Scum" follow with a sound seeped in Brooklyn bite, songs that sneer at exhaustion and self-doubt but can't quite hide their weariness. A dance through disillusionment.

    Then everything softens. "Someone Great" is where the record ﬁnally cracks open. An ode to his late therapist, a tender steady pulse and soaring synths smother Murphy's vocals, even at its most vulnerable, the album is begging you to dance. James speaks about loss, memory, and the unbearable normalcy of the world moving on. It's one of those songs that always makes me cry, losing the person you were most vulnerable with, and aching for the comfort you can no longer ﬁnd in them. A nihilistic malaise about the world ﬂoods your brain, and the simple details of life wash away in irrelevance to the pain you're feeling. A personal favorite of mine from Murphy:

    "The worst is all the lovely weather
    I'm stunned it's not raining
    The coffee isn't even bitter
    Because, what's the difference?"

    Somehow Murphy keeps this existential danceparty going on "All My Friends," which takes that ache and turns it into something transcendent. A pounding piano, relentless and bright, carries Murphy's voice as he looks back at years spent chasing meaning through noise and movement. It's hopeful and tragic all at once, a song about growing older, losing people, and realizing how rare it is to still feel connected. Another song that will always make me tear up, to Murphy his mistakes, the loss, the pain, was all worth it for his true life passion: music.

    The album softly closes on "New York, I Love You but You're Bringing Me Down," a bittersweet love ballad to his home NYC with my favorite vocal delivery on the album and a dramatic crescendo of piano, guitar drums and Murphys crooning poetry. A perfect way to end a perfect album.

    Sound of Silver is a dance-punk opus that will leave you crying for as long as it keeps you dancing. While its inﬂuences from the likes of Eno, Aphex Twin, and Talking Heads is potent, Murphy's tender lyrics, sassy and ironic delivery, and masterfully composed grooves allow this album to transcend far beyond its roots. While the sound is tinged with silver, its core is nothing but gold.
