Find of the Week: Tetris Oscilloscope!

The find of the week this week is an HP 54603B oscilloscope, purely for its Tetris capability. If you find one yourself in the wild, press Print Utility, then the 2nd and 3rd from left buttons under the screen at the same time, and enjoy yourself a round (or 20) of Tetris! You can even use the knobs to rotate/move the piece. I love Easter eggs like that!

Wait a minute... where are the tech dumps?

If you're asking this question, you've come to the right place! We've gotten some requests from people to finally show where you can find the tech dumps. This handy dandy guide will show you where!

JEC Dump
This one's usually the most active. If you go into the JEC via the 2nd floor doors, swing left by the elevators and go straight through the fire door. Go straight again and you should see 2 large cardboard boxes.
DCC Dump
This one's sometimes active. If you enter from the DCC entrance with the vending machines, go straight into the white hallway, and take a right. Keep going right and you should find a kind of loading dock with tech dump goodies!
VCC Dump
This is a very much right-place, right-time kinda tech dump. If you head into the VCC and go left, and straight down the stairwell and through the door, there's a box at the end of the hall. You can also take the elevator down and it's right there in the basement floor. Note: This dump closes after 5.
Low Dump
Not many seem to know about this one. It's pretty out of the way. Entering from the entrance closest to the 15th Street bridge, take an immediate left. Follow the hallway down to about the middle, then take a right. You should pass a freight elevator and some bathrooms. Take a left here, and you should see some blast doors. Keep going. There are sometimes tech dump boxes in this hallway. If you go all the way you should see 2 boxes that are always there. That, lo(w) and behold, is Low.
Cogswell/MRC
These are typically very randomly active, but when they are there's usually a lot of cool stuff. For cogswell you need to go past the men's restroom, and into this room that is labeled ‘receiving.' Go further through this black door, and on your left should be the tech dump.
Sage/West
This one almost never has anything deposited, but sometimes worth a look. Go down the elevator of sage and those recycling bins by the vending machines are actually tech dump, not regular recycling bins. West is on the 1st floor of west. You should see it. There's usually no change for a while.

Hope this helped you all! If you want a floor-plan-map guide, it's pinned in the RPI free and for sale Discord server's #tech-dump channel!

Tunnel Vision: Out of the Tunnel

DCC 308, A Case Study

—By West Virginia

DCC 308. The quintessential RPI lecture hall. Chances are you've had at least one class in this room during your time here. My first ever class at RPI was Intro to Biology in DCC 308. Upon first glance DCC 308 is a regular lecture hall set up with uniform yellow plaster chairs. In reality it's a regular lecture hall with non-uniform yellow plaster chairs!

Back in 1973 when the DCC was first built, 308 had one less row of seats, a bar separating students from the lecture floor, and the lecture floor was about a foot lower. The lecture hall is comprised of three types of yellow plaster seats. Type One features a fully square back and square seat. Type Two features a slightly rounded back, no longer fully square, with a seat that is a slightly more rounded version of the Type One seat. Honorary mention for the only non-yellow seat. A gray colored Type Two seat can be found in the upper rightmost corner of 308, when facing the room from the lecture stand. Type Three features the most triangular back of the three. The type three bottom also features less of a concave shape when compared to Types One and Two.

So what seat should you choose? Right away you should know Type Three chairs are generally thinner than the others and will offer limited back support. That being said, if you like bouncing back and forth in your seat, a Type Three chair might just be exactly what you're looking for. Type one chairs feature a bottom with a comparatively larger surface area if you like a large seat, but the lip of the chair paired with the depth of the seat can result in cut-off blood circulation to the legs when seated for extended periods of time. Type Two are my personal favorite. The perfect depth and lip will leave you feeling comfortably secure in the chair without your legs falling asleep, and the back is sturdy without being too hard. So now armed with this information, I trust you will pick the best seat for you!
