This find of the week was pretty nuts. I didn't even know they made motors, electric motors, this big. It was at least 3 bananas tall, if you laid them vertically tip to tail, and the shaft was at least 1.5 bananas thick. It's in Low right now probably if you want to see for yourself!

Failed Drug Delivery Rains Drugs on RPI Campus

On Monday, November 3rd, the RPI campus was hit with a mysterious white sprinkling. Many wondered why this powder was raining down all of a sudden.

People were seen running in circles in a panic, unsure of what to do with themselves. Cars were dusted, the grass was all powdery white. What could it be, experts wondered.

The answer came when someone tasted this powder. It was drugs. All of it was drugs, falling from the sky, due to a cargo plane full of them losing all its cargo mid-flight.

Soon enough word spread, and within days of this powdering, most if not all of this powder mysteriously disappeared. One can only speculate what happened with this powder. One can only wonder.

The Best Water Fountain on Campus

—By West Virginia

Okay so here's the deal. I drink water, you drink water. There are so many water fountains across campus, but which is best? There are over 70 buildings located on Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute's Troy NY campus. From residence halls to the athletic facilities, that's just too many buildings. I don't have access to a bunch of those buildings and neither do you, most likely. Maybe you do have access to those special buildings, but guess what, I don't care because I don't have access and I'm the author. As the author I have decided that I will be limiting my scope to the academic campus. This way most everyone reading this will be able to benefit from the findings. There are 23 buildings that make up the academics & research facilities here. I will be excluding certain buildings dedicated solely to research (lab buildings) or for graduate studies, and including certain buildings like the Union and Academy hall which are categorized as student life buildings. This leaves us with the following list of 15 buildings:

  • Amos Eaton
  • Carnegie
  • Center for Industrial Innovation, Low (CII)
  • Darrin Communications Center (DCC)
  • Jonsson Engineering Center
  • Folsom Library
  • Lally Hall
  • Pittsburg Building
  • Ricketts Building
  • Russell Sage Laboratory
  • Jonsson-Rowland Science Center (J-Rowl)
  • Voorhees Computing Center (VCC)
  • Walker Laboratory
  • West Hall
  • Troy Building

Subterranean Homesick Blues

—by π

Indie supergroup boygenius' self-titled EP feels like huddling in the after-hours glow of a vintage jukebox, three friends bleeding their hearts into a microphone in the corner of a basement club. It balances on the knife's edge of innocence and intensity, like a comforting hug after feeling alone for too long. The trio (Julien Baker, Phoebe Bridgers, and Lucy Dacus) each bring their own scars, weaving them into six songs that sound like late-night confessions. By turns folky, raucous, and quietly epic, the EP could be the soundtrack to your most vulnerable moments.

The name boygenius itself is a coy wink and upside-down joke at all the overzealous "boy geniuses" that we all had the (dis)pleasure of meeting. These three grew up in small towns and quiet churches, raised on heavy silences and hidden truths, and formed a bond on subtle guitar riffs and silly giggles late into the night.

In "Bite the Hand," Lucy Dacus's rich contralto sings against a steady guitar, the notes pulsing like a defiant heartbeat. By the chorus all three voices converge on the refrain "I can't love you how you want me to," turning a quiet confession into a impassioned testimony. What starts as a whisper becomes a collective cathartic scream.

With "Me & My Dog," Phoebe Bridgers takes the mic with her trademark deadpan lyricism. The song sounds sweet and jangly, but the humor is bitter: every line lands like a clever barb at loneliness. Phoebe's soft drawl half-smiles through heartbreak, making solitude sound like the punchline of an inside joke.

"Souvenir" is the song I'm quietly obsessed with. A love confession from someone too insecure and somber to know if they are capable of love, or being loved. It floats somberly along in a cloudy haze, tender and hesitant, like a feeling you wake up from and wish you could fall back into.

In "Stay Down," Julien Baker continues this theme by reflecting on her feelings of isolation. Guitars swirl around her vocals like a cyclone, chords heavy under the weight of her pain. "Push me down into the water… hold me under," she cries, unable to properly cope with the breakup she stays in the murk that the emptiness has left her in.

Then "Salt in the Wound," led by Lucy Dacus, plunges everything right back in. Her guitar work slices through the air and her voice matches the acidity in her lyricism. "They say the Hearts and Minds are on your side…what they don't say is what's on your side." Her callous honesty perfectly harmonizes with the raw instrumentation that swells behind her.

Finally, "Ketchum, ID" closes the EP in a wash of calm. It's a road-trip lullaby by Phoebe: just guitar, harmonies, and a gentle pedal steel. They dream of escaping to snowy hills and quiet cabins—"so far from where we've been," their vocals promise, finally a place to catch your breath. It feels like a calm bus ride home through Massachusetts or a warm hug after crying from homesickness; soft, melancholy, and undeniably sincere.

What makes boygenius' EP so powerful is its vulnerability. Its intimacy is sometimes overwhelming, like every word is written in cursive and smudged with tear stains. The boys' emotional bond is evident, three voices turning loneliness into something shared. It's friendship blazing bright in the dark: an anthem for anyone who's ever whispered their true self into the night.

Extra: Battle of the Bands 11/07: A Fun Night!

—by I

If you found yourself in the Union between 7–10:30 PM on 11/07, you were in for a treat. The Battle of the Bands was on! In all, there were 7 bands: Super Critical, Gamma Radiation, Revitalizer, Guts, Entropy, Pseudosky, and Totally Confused.

The whole thing is on RPI TV's YouTube channel if you want to watch it back for yourself if you missed it. Plus, The Poly released an article on the Battle of the Bands as well! This is the only paper coverage of the event, though!

Unfortunately, we weren't able to catch the entire show, but what we did see was quite fun and enjoyable. Here are 3 of the highlights! Coming in right as Revitalizer was ending their set, the crowd was relatively energetic. When Guts came on, and invited everyone to come closer to the stage, that's when the concert energy picked up even more. Given Guts is a metal band, there were lots of people headbanging, and on top of that, there were multiple mosh pits and circle pits that started throughout the course of their set. Fun times!

The next band, Entropy, had some commotion in the form of posters. Two people ran around the dance floor holding huge cardboard signs, one saying “We love the bassist from Entropy,” and the other saying “We hate the bassist from Entropy,” and they even staged a little scuffle up front before running back. During this set someone was also lifted up in a chair, overall also a fun time.

The final moment we'll touch on was Totally Confused's beginning. With a turtle plushie in hand, the lead singer raised this plush to the sky like Rafiki raised Simba, albeit a bit more energetically. Also a bunch of times. And the best part was the crowd cheered every time the turtle was lifted. According to The Poly, this turtle was their mascot, Squirt. The whole thing reminded me of the SpongeBob talent show episode where SpongeBob mopped the floor and the crowd cheered when SpongeBob mopped up, but only when SpongeBob was on stage.

As mentioned earlier, we weren't able to catch the entire concert for ourselves, but from watching back RPI TV's live coverage on YouTube, we were able to find the winners: in 3rd, Pseudosky; in 2nd, Guts; and the winner of the 2025 UPAC Battle of the Bands, Totally Confused!

If you haven't been this year, be sure to come to next year's Battle of the Bands! It's a great opportunity to get out, forget about homework and classes for a few hours, and support your fellow students as they play their music!
