What's new in the box(es): Literally new boxes, that's what

—By I

Low Goes Crazy on Friday

Sometimes, I lose hope that anything interesting will show up somewhere like Low, when nothing new shows up there for weeks at times. Then I go to Low on an unsuspecting Friday and find 10 or even 11 boxes of things waiting to be looked through.

This week was pretty slow until Friday came through like a freight train. It appears that they emptied/replaced all the boxes (except for what seems like MRC/Cogswell/West's boxes) and just dumped 'em into Low. All the tech dump items you've seen for the past few weeks have found themselves in Low, in other words. Plus boxes that weren't before accessible, it seems.

A box from the VCC was spotted being hauled away to a truck outside around 2 PM on Friday. It had tons of really old Sun PCs and Dell Optiplexes (model GS, really old). Checking Low only a few hours before would have shown you nothing, checking around 5 or 6 would have shown you the literal motherload of stuff. Plus some new things that haven't been in the tech dumps before! If you're in the mood for an office photocopier, there are 2 in Low as of Friday. They're about 4 feet tall, and have a sign telling you to recycle them. There's probably a good chance they work, though I haven't tested them myself.

Besides that day, Tuesday also had some JEC finds, you can read about that and the less exciting finds below in What's Actually New.

Find of the Week: CRT TV/VCR/DVD Combo, Big PC Case, Old Sun PCs

I couldn't decide on just one find of the week, there was so much to choose from! The first find of the week this week was the CRT TV/VCR/DVD combo, since it worked perfectly, had vivid colors, and allowed us to see what was actually on some of the VHS tapes from the DCC. And to our surprise, it was not a bunch of spicy videos, it was actually a lot of old TV recordings! By the time I checked Low later on, the TV was already taken, but I'll never forget the precious time spent with it. The second find of the week was the biggest PC case I've ever seen in my life in Low, and the third multiple Sun PCs. I love the old "under-monitor" form factor the really old PCs have, and there were a lot of them.

State of the Art Extra: Cameras installed everywhere!

—By I

Hours before going to press, I was taking one last check through the tech dumps and tunnels on Sunday night only to find that there was paint. They used black spray paint on all the art this time in the JEC–DCC tunnel. And in half of the JEC–JROWL tunnel. Blue paint on the JROWL tunnel walls. Everywhere. Upon looking up at the ceiling, I saw cameras. Everywhere. I counted 11 cameras total. I am sad to report that the large art pieces documented last week have been completely painted over. In either black or blue. Mona Lisa and the Pink Floyd "paintings" in the JEC–JROWL tunnels have been painted over, as well. You can still see some of it through the paint, but it is quite a tragic loss. In addition to this, the long-standing "The lighter makes a spark but I look better in the dark" words have also been removed. When I say everything, it's truly all of the prominent art (some of the tiny art in more remote places has been left, as well as chalk art). The only art that has survived this mass painting was some elevator art or small pieces scattered around. It is unclear what, if anything, is going to happen to the art in other parts of campus.

The only outside source of information that may pertain to this situation at hand was a news submission anonymously sent in to us last semester. This may have been information related to the camera installed in the JEC tunnel over the summer, however it may also relate to this event, as well. The submitter, who wished to remain anonymous, stated:

"I heard from a friend of a friend who ran into a maintenance worker at the tunnels that cameras are going to be put up soon, one at each end. We speculate that it might be due to the amount of spray paint and graffiti not on the tunnel walls this year, since that can't be easily painted over. Lots of new art has been sprayed on the floor and drawn on the bare concrete walls farther in the tunnel. Who knows what this means for the future of the state of the art?"

While mostly speculative, this information may at least show that the tunnels may have started to be more closely looked at by the admins or maintenance workers. Again, this is all speculation and unconfirmed, but no other information has yet been gathered at this time. If we get more information by next week, we will be sure to share it with you all.

State of the Art: So Much... Paint?

—By I

Yikes. It's hard to say why, but in the past week the JEC–DCC tunnel has been repainted thrice. By the time Issue #6 came out last Monday, all the art from the JEC–DCC tunnel in that issue was gone (except the faces inside the holes, last week's big art was not in this section of tunnel). You can still see some remnants of them if you look deep into the paint. Between Tuesday and Wednesday, the tunnel was repainted again, only this time they used gray paint instead of white, for some reason. You can see the gray rectangles if you go there now. Over the weekend there was a third repainting as well as mass camera installation. You can read more about that on page 3.

We will see what the future holds for State of the Art. I'll try to gather as much as possible, but if this keeps happening it might be harder to find stuff to document. In any case, hope you enjoy this week's pictures, there were a lot!

Subterranean Homesick Blues

Life Abuse

—By π

Capital Region's Crust Punk Supergroup Life Abuse's 2024 debut LP Systemization is a nostalgia trip packed with jagged guitar riffs, sporadic drum strikes, chugging bass, and venom-laced vocal deliveries.

The band is a long-overdue reunion of former "Deathsquad" members Mark (main vocals/guitar), Nate Wilson (guitar), and Dan Barker (drums). Bassist Sean Doody joins the collective, rekindling a fire that first sparked over a quarter-century ago.

The album drips with disdain for human corruption—both institutional and psychological. On the title track "Systemization" Mark's voice bellows over relentless beats and anxious, infectious riffs, narrating a wasteland of mortality:

"Statues shatter into pieces, flags slashed and burned in the shadows of the hunger circus, new bastards take their fun."

Another standout, the instrumental "Threnody" feels like it was unearthed straight from Dante's descent through hell. Its trudging riffs would be just as comfortable on a Magrudergrind record as it is here, with bass and drums pounding like funeral rites for the living and a dirge for the damned.

Throughout, the band scorches media propaganda and the fascist agendas it feeds. Nowhere is this more potent than the closing track "No Perspective" where Mark snarls against the media's weaponization of ignorance: shoveling hate into the mouths of the starving and impressionable. Between passages, the instruments twist from breakneck blast beats into luminous riffs and pulsing rhythms—a brutal dance of chaos and clarity. It's the kind of cohesion only decades of scars and sweat can conjure.

Life Abuse hasn't just released an album; they've carved a manifesto in distortion. Systemization is a world so warped by abuse of power that reality itself bends to its decay—a soundtrack for the collapse, delivered with unflinching rage.

I implore you to listen to the album and experience the deluge for yourself. Go see Life Abuse at Troy's very own No Fun on September 17th. Think Irrationally!
