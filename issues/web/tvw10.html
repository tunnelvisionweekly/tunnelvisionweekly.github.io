    A Nightmare Day in the Life

    —By West Virginia

    You wake up drenched in sweat to sound of your roommate's alarm. They won't get out of bed for another hour at least, but nonetheless a new alarm will go off just about every 5 minutes. You roll out of bed and immediately trip over your roommate's shoes, which have been left in the middle of the room. It's 9 and you have Intro to Bio at 10, so you decide to stop at Commons for a quick bite. You wait in line behind a kid wearing cat ears and a tail for some sausage and soggy eggs. While you're eating you check your CS1 midterm grade: 34%. The average was 52%. Distracted by this news, you don't notice it's almost 10. You get coffee to go and hurry off to class. It snowed last night so the steps are extra slippery and you slip almost instantly. You land hard on your ass and the coffee goes flying. The snow soaked through your pants and you're officially late.

    Finally, you get to DCC 308 and suddenly remember that today is your group presentation. You're the second group to go, leaving you just enough time to jot down some talking points before it's your turn to go up. Your presentation is on CRISPR. So is the next group's—theirs was better. You forgot today was Thursday and this week bears and berries is due. You spend the rest of Bio trying to fix your buggy code with no luck. The last group finishes their presentation at 11:30 and you think you might get out early, so you pack up, but the professor holds you till 12, talking about the final.

    Your next class is in Pittsburgh. The snow has turned to ice and you start to lose your footing just as a tour group comes out of sage labs. They watch you eat shit for the second time in 3 hours. Once you arrive at the doors you run up to the second floor sweaty and out of breath, only to realise class was canceled—the professor sent out an email at 11:40. Defeated, you sit in the empty room staring at your laptop, partially relieved that you'll have more time to work on your code and partially pissed that you have no excuse not to anymore. Again you try and fail to fix your buggy code.

    It's only been an hour but you're about to smash your ThinkPad on the floor so you decide to hike back up to Sage, ravenous by the time you arrive. All they have is hotdogs with stale buns. But the waffle making stuff is still out so you try and make a waffle when one of the Sage workers comes out and yells at you. She says that waffles are only for breakfast and it's 1 PM. She lets you finish making the waffle but you don't even want it anymore. You get a soft-serve swirl and eat it shivering as you walk back to your dorm. You decide you're either gonna take a nap or work on your code but you can't do either because your roommate is watching TikTok on full volume. Instead you take a shower and try to clear your head.

    Feeling clean and refreshed you walk to the library to lock in but after searching every floor the only open seat you find is on the first floor in the makeshift classroom area. Just as you set up your laptop, charger, and notebook, students start filing in and you realise a class is about to begin. As the space fills up, you debate staying there while class is in session, but once the professor starts talking, you decide against it. Everyone, including the professor, gives you a weird look as you pack up and leave.

    CS Office hours are about to start so you get in the queue while you're still in the library and walk over to Lally. There was no one in the queue when you joined so your name had already been called while you weren't there and they skipped over you. By the time you're sitting in Lally with Submitty back open there are already 50 people in the queue. After 3 hours a TA calls your name, but you went to the bathroom so they skipped you again. You recognize some kids from your dorm. One of them is already in Data Structures as a first-semester freshman and you wonder what he's doing there. He's in shorts and flip-flops even though it's November and only 14 degrees outside. He asks to look at your code and proceeds to explain what you already know—why it's not working—but is unable to actually offer a solution to the problem. Despite this he manages to leave feeling smarter than you and asks why you decided to major in computer science in the first place. You're wondering this yourself as you walk back home in the dark and try to motivate yourself to keep working on this code.

    After a few more hours, midnight finally comes and you got 30/100 points but at least you can justify going to bed because you have no more late days!

    Geologic Fever

    Forensic science is not just for the police!

    —by S. S. Illite

    An important topic for an important issue. Welcome to the world of being an Earth scientist!

    Much of the Earth sciences rely on forensics and your ability to deduct as much as you can from what is given to you. Geologists have to rely on all of their senses (literally!) to do their most effective identification.

    The Mohs hardness scale is a 1—10 scale that helps scientists determine the strength of a material compared to other items. Depending on how you use it, and what you use it to identify, you can discover lots of identifiable information about the rock or mineral you are identifying!

    Here is a simple text version of the scale. On the right is the Mohs numerical value, and the middle column compares "everyday items" (let's be real, how many of you are familiar with mica?) to the minerals on the left.
    Mineral Everyday Item Hardness
    Talc 1
    Gypsum 2
    Fingernail 2.5
    Calcite Mica 3
    Copper coin 3.2
    Fluorite 4
    Apatite Glass 5
    Knife blade, nail 5.5
    Feldspar 6
    Steel file 6.5
    Quartz Sandpaper 7
    Topaz 8
    Corundum 9
    Diamond 10

    Talc is very soft and often called soapstone. Most diamonds you see are cut or otherwise processed, which gives them their distinct shapes and defined angles. Like many gemstones, diamonds start out rough! These two are harder to tell apart by color, but as any baby Earth scientist learns, color is misleading. Things like strength, reaction to hydrochloric acid (HCl), growth patterns, and streak color are much more defining.

    "Streak" has many definitions, and trust me, geologists are not the ones you need to worry about ditching their pants to identify rocks. A streak is a powder residue left over on a streak plate when a rock/mineral is dragged across it. These plates are made out of unglazed porcelain or silica, and can come in black or white. Hardness-wise, they are a 6.5 on the Mohs scale. Sometimes this streak isn't even close to the same color as the actual rock/mineral it came from! So cool!

    If you're interested in learning more, the National Park Service is a great resource. Check out their article here!

    Subterranean Homesick Blues

    —by π

    LCD Soundsystem is the musical collective and love child of multi-instrumentalist, singer-songwriter, DJ, and producer James Murphy. On the band's sophomore album Sound of Silver, Murphy, along with his tight-knit group of collaborators, builds something that feels both deeply personal yet seeped in Murphy's own inﬂuences, like a love letter made of all his favorite records. Murphy has never shied from where he draws his inﬂuences from, but his meticulous curation of new wave, funk, punk, and house, or as he calls it, "silver music," gives the album its unmistakable pulse. The whole thing is soaked in groovy baselines, wiry distorted synths, and rhythms that sound equally at home on a hi-ﬁ setup in a Brooklyn loft or in a dingy smoke-ﬁlled club in Cologne.

    There's something almost cruel about how Sound of Silver balances irony and sincerity. It's Murphy at his most self-aware, funny, and devastating. The record feels like the hangover at the end of your 30s, the realization that chasing cool doesn't make you feel alive anymore and groovy baselines and driving drums are all that's left to keep you moving.

    "Get Innocuous!" opens the record like a trance, all shimmering synths and mechanical pulse, slowly building until it devours all of the space around you. With haunting montras it perfectly sets the tone for the record. "Time to Get Away" and "North American Scum" follow with a sound seeped in Brooklyn bite, songs that sneer at exhaustion and self-doubt but can't quite hide their weariness. A dance through disillusionment.

    Then everything softens. "Someone Great" is where the record ﬁnally cracks open. An ode to his late therapist, a tender steady pulse and soaring synths smother Murphy's vocals, even at its most vulnerable, the album is begging you to dance. James speaks about loss, memory, and the unbearable normalcy of the world moving on. It's one of those songs that always makes me cry, losing the person you were most vulnerable with, and aching for the comfort you can no longer ﬁnd in them. A nihilistic malaise about the world ﬂoods your brain, and the simple details of life wash away in irrelevance to the pain you're feeling. A personal favorite of mine from Murphy:

    "The worst is all the lovely weather
    I'm stunned it's not raining
    The coffee isn't even bitter
    Because, what's the difference?"

    Somehow Murphy keeps this existential danceparty going on "All My Friends," which takes that ache and turns it into something transcendent. A pounding piano, relentless and bright, carries Murphy's voice as he looks back at years spent chasing meaning through noise and movement. It's hopeful and tragic all at once, a song about growing older, losing people, and realizing how rare it is to still feel connected. Another song that will always make me tear up, to Murphy his mistakes, the loss, the pain, was all worth it for his true life passion: music.

    The album softly closes on "New York, I Love You but You're Bringing Me Down," a bittersweet love ballad to his home NYC with my favorite vocal delivery on the album and a dramatic crescendo of piano, guitar drums and Murphys crooning poetry. A perfect way to end a perfect album.

    Sound of Silver is a dance-punk opus that will leave you crying for as long as it keeps you dancing. While its inﬂuences from the likes of Eno, Aphex Twin, and Talking Heads is potent, Murphy's tender lyrics, sassy and ironic delivery, and masterfully composed grooves allow this album to transcend far beyond its roots. While the sound is tinged with silver, its core is nothing but gold.
