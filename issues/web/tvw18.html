Tunnel Vision: Out of the Tunnel

The Living Tombstone

—By West Virginia

We're taking a break from our regularly scheduled water fountain programming for a very special review of this year's spring concert. It was amazing. Truly. From start to finish it was one of the best concerts I've been to in awhile. The opener was a duo called Boy Jr. and the main performance was a band called The Living Tombstone.

The electro pop duo Boy Jr. had amazing energy and they're bound to blow up soon. They played a few of their newer songs like "Anxious Attachment Avoidant Attachment," which had a fun call-and-response that had everyone in the audience screaming at the top of their lungs. They also played a brand new song just released last month, "I LOVE OTHER PEOPLE EVEN IF I DON'T KNOW THEM." Definitely worth checking them out on Spotify (@Boy Jr.) or anywhere else where you can stream music.

Okay so if I'm being completely honest I only really started listening to The Living Tombstone when I found out they were coming to RPI for this year's spring concert. That being said, I had heard them before, I knew a few of their most popular songs, "Discord" and "No Mercy" for starters, but that's just because I have a phone with Internet access. In preparation for the concert I listened to whatever Apple Music listed as their most popular songs and then eventually just shuffled their whole discography. I can see how it might not be for everyone but I really enjoyed their whole rock, EDM, pop combination. This was their first ever concert on a college campus, and Rust (the green one) even drew Puckman for us while Zero One (the orange one) took over vocals. The whole band had such a great stage presence. I actually can't imagine how hot it must have been up on that stage in those bulky suits, but thankfully UPAC Concerts were blasting the air conditioning in ECAV.

My personal recommendations for getting into their music is as follows: "My Ordinary Life" (arguably their most famous song), "Step On Up," "Jump Up Super Star," "I Wanna Be A Machine," and "Be Alone." A nice mix of old and new stuff!

Subterranean Homesick Blues

—by π

If there was ever a record that felt like it was made not in a studio but in a bunker, with the walls closing in and the air thinning out, it'd be Mezzanine. Massive Attack have always been architects of mood, but here it feels like they stopped designing homes and started creating monoliths; enormous, alien, and cold. As the story goes, 3D and Daddy G were barely speaking. Mushroom, the group's third pillar, essentially walked out midway through, his contributions either scrapped or buried beneath layers of guitar distortion he never signed off on. What remained was a record forged in toxicity and stubbornness as two men pulled a sound in opposite directions until the tension itself became the point. If Blue Lines was a conversation and Protection was a seduction, Mezzanine is an argument that neither side is willing to lose.

"Angel" opens the record as a perfect tone setter for what's to come. That bassline (you already know the one) swallows the atmosphere in any room that it's played in. You feel it in your molars before you register it as music. Horace Andy's falsetto floats over the top like a spirit caught between planes, too pure for the wreckage underneath him. "Risingson" follows and strips things even further back, 3D half-rapping, half-confessing over a beat that sounds like a migraine set to a metronome. "Teardrop" is, of course, the centerpiece. Elizabeth Fraser's vocal hovers somewhere between a lullaby and a last rite. The harpsichord loop is extremely delicate and adds a reprieve for the moody soundscape. This coupled with the lyrical vulnerability on display makes it easy to see why it's the band's most iconic track.

"Inertia Creeps" in my opinion stands as one of the records most underappreciated tracks. It's a slow crawl where the deranged 3D whispers about surveillance over a beat that shivers and twitches like its laced with amphetamine. "Exchange" and "Dissolved Girl" sit in the album's midsection like sediment, thick and intoxicating, Sara Jay's voice on the latter adding a narcotic haze that makes the whole thing feel like sinking into deep murky water with no hope of resurfacing. "Group Four" and "Black Milk" close the record in a wash of strings and dread, a perfect tonal conclusion to this moody swan song to the group as we knew them. The collaborator list reads like a deliberately curated conflux of artistic minds: Elizabeth Fraser of the Cocteau Twins lending her otherworldly instrument to a record that couldn't be further from Heaven or Las Vegas, Horace Andy returning as the group's spiritual anchor, and a relatively unknown Sara Jay delivering performances that rival either of them. But the secret weapon might be Angelo Badalamenti's orchestral arrangements, the man who scored David Lynch's filmography lending his cinematic unease is the cherry on top for the records brooding atmosphere.

This album doesn't feel like it should work. It's bleak, slow, and hostile. The band was actively disintegrating while they made it and the production is so dense it borders on claustrophobic. Nothing they made before had this weight. Yet it's hard not to feel like the album was made not in spite of domestic conflict but as a child of it. It's tension manifested into sound waves.

From the Desk of Carnivalesque

—by carnivalesque

Birds chirping, flowers blooming, classes slamming us with work. Spring is the season for change and new beginnings; for you, dear reader, transformation is imminent. Enjoy my last words before I move on to the big lecture hall in the sky…

Dear Carnivalesque, my son is an incoming freshman planning to dual major in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering with a CS minor. My darling boy has a 4.3 GPA and got a 1580 on the SAT. He's planning to do research his first semester and join a couple of engineering clubs, do you have any recommendations? What are the classes like? Should I hire him a private tutor? Also, my baby boy has never lived alone before. Is there a laundry service I can hire? Will his RA wake him up and warm up a glass of milk before he goes to bed? And where can parents sleep in the dorm when I visit every week? I'm missing his sweet precious face already.

—Manic Over Moving My Youngest

Dear M.O.M.M.Y., your son is going to fail out of RPI. You think that a 4.3 GPA and 1580 is going to cut it here? He'll barely make C's in classes here. I would recommend him doing research under Dr. Tysoe (he does great work in Thermite) and join EWB (Enginees Within Buildings), the urban exploration club. No need for a laundry service, many freshman dorms will dump out the laundry from the machine if he doesn't get it quick enough. As for living accommodations, the study rooms in Barton are always open! When your son eventually stops calling you every day, it's because he got kidnapped and you need to worry. Good luck, and we'll see you in August!

—carnivalesque

Comic of the Day

The Inaugural Touch Grass Music Festival

Organized by GZ Basement, WRPI, and Ski and Snowboard Club

—By I

Did you go to the RPI Touch Grass Music Festival on April 11th? If you didn't, you missed out! Don't worry though, we here at Tunnel Vision don't want you to feel left out, so here's a little recap!

It all started in the wee hours of the morning for the staff. Turns out they were there from around 5 or 6 AM all the way to 3 AM the next day for setup and takedown. The main events started at 3, though. And the music went from ~4:30 to ~11. I for one wasn't expecting to stay longer than a few hours, but I ended up being there from 3 all the way to the end of the last set!

There was lots to do at this festival, besides the obvious pastime of touching grass. If you weren't there for music, there were 2 spots for chalk drawings and a table covered in paper for people to draw on with crayons, tons of crafts vendors, swag as well of course, and plenty of other games and food you could choose from! Some of the promotional material said there were over 40 booths, and I'll believe it! Some of my favorites were the DDR stand, Ben and Jerry's unexpected return via ice cream cart, the WRPI stand with lots of free CDs, and the various food vendors (though some prices were a bit steep, looking at you $10 hot dog). Other fun things to do included carnival games with free prizes, Habitat For Humanity's nail hammering setup, RPI Game Dev Club's custom arcade cabinet, and a whole lot of crafts you could buy from other fellow students!

Music-wise, it was cool to see the crowd ebb and flow as the day turned into night. At the start, there was actually nobody dancing. The pit in front of the stage was completely empty, save for a few people. Most were sitting down, ahem, touching grass. As time went on, however, the sitting crowd thinned out and the pit crowd grew. It reached a crescendo during Pseudosky's set (my personal favorite!), and after that the crowd shrank to perhaps half of what it was by the time Mall Goth, the last band, played. One of my favorite crowd things, besides the numerous mosh pits was a game of Simon Says during Pseudosky's set. Haven't seen that one before!

I personally feel like RPI needs a lot more of these kinds of events. I haven't seen such a congregation of students since that one guy ate an entire rotisserie chicken in front of Sage last school year, and it's a great feeling to be around such a horde of familiar people. You never know when you'll bump into someone you know!

There was so much going on at this festival it was hard to squeeze it all into one page. If you have any photos of the event you'd like to share, send them to us and we may feature it in a future edition or our website!

Extra Extra: Tunnel Art is Back (for the 3rd time)!

—by I

Right before sitting down to do finishing touches on this issue today, I walked into the JEC–DCC tunnels to find the walls looked a bit whiter than before. Then I looked again, did a double take, and whipped out my phone so fast it started smoking. Tunnel art is back, thanks to the Student Senate, and it's so much better than I could've imagined! The entire length of the tunnel wall is covered in big sheets of paper, and markers and crayons next to the DCC entrance let you get your drawing fix without needing to BYOP. The best part, to me, is that it's official, it's been approved (it seems), as the Senate Communications & Engagement Committee and Student Senate have their name written all over it.
